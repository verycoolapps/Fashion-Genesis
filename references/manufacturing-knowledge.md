# Manufacturing Knowledge: From Concept to Product

Reference module for Fashion Genesis X. Covers prototyping tiers, construction techniques, hardware sourcing, production models, quality control, cost drivers, sustainable manufacturing, and complexity ratings.

Status convention: **[VERIFIED]** = established practice, **[EXPERIMENTAL]** = emerging method, **[SPECULATIVE]** = unproven approach.

---

## 1. Prototyping Tiers

Prototyping is the bridge between concept and production. Fashion prototyping has distinct tiers, each serving a different purpose.

### 1.1 Tier 1: Concept / Cardboard / Paper Prototypes

**Purpose:** Validate form, proportion, and basic function before investing in materials.

**Materials:**
- Cardboard, foam board, paper
- Tape, glue, staples
- Basic sewing (paper/foam)
- 3D printing (FDM, SLA) for complex shapes

**What it tests:**
- Overall silhouette and proportion
- Basic ergonomics (does it fit the body?)
- Structural feasibility (can it be built?)
- Visual impact (does it look right?)

**Timeline:** Hours to days
**Cost:** $0-$50
**Limitations:** No fabric behavior, no real materials, no wear testing

**Example:** Cardboard backpack mockup to test strap placement and load distribution.

### 1.2 Tier 2: Muslin / Calico / Toile Prototypes

**Purpose:** Test fit, construction, and pattern accuracy in cheap fabric.

**Materials:**
- Muslin (unbleached cotton)
- Calico (plain weave cotton)
- Cotton drill (heavy cotton)
- Canvas (for bags/outerwear)
- Basic sewing machine

**What it tests:**
- Fit and pattern accuracy
- Construction methods
- Seam placement and finishing
- Drape and movement (basic)
- Pattern grading (if multi-size)

**Timeline:** Days to weeks
**Cost:** $50-$500
**Limitations:** No final materials, no color, no texture, limited functionality

**Example:** Muslin jacket to test pattern fit, sleeve cap ease, and collar construction.

### 1.3 Tier 3: Sample / Proto Sample

**Purpose:** Test in actual or near-actual materials; validate design and function.

**Materials:**
- Target fabrics (or close substitutes)
- Actual hardware (zippers, buckles, etc.)
- Trims and accessories
- Production sewing techniques

**What it tests:**
- Material behavior (drape, stretch, durability)
- Color and print accuracy
- Hardware fit and function
- Construction quality
- Wear and tear (basic)
- User experience

**Timeline:** Weeks to months
**Cost:** $500-$5,000
**Limitations:** Still not production-scale, limited quantity

**Example:** Sample backpack in actual Cordura with Fidlock buckles to test function and durability.

### 1.4 Tier 4: Pre-Production / PP Sample

**Purpose:** Validate production readiness; test manufacturing process.

**Materials:**
- Exact production materials
- Exact hardware and trims
- Production sewing/assembly methods
- Production packaging

**What it tests:**
- Manufacturing feasibility
- Quality control checkpoints
- Cost accuracy
- Lead time
- Scalability
- Packaging and labeling

**Timeline:** Months
**Cost:** $5,000-$50,000
**Limitations:** Still limited quantity (typically 10-100 units)

**Example:** PP sample run of 50 backpacks to validate production process and quality.

### 1.5 Tier 5: Pilot Production / Small Batch

**Purpose:** Test market response; validate business model.

**Materials:**
- Production materials
- Production methods
- Production packaging
- Retail-ready product

**What it tests:**
- Market demand
- Pricing and positioning
- Customer feedback
- Manufacturing consistency
- Quality at scale
- Logistics and fulfillment

**Timeline:** Months to years
**Cost:** $50,000-$500,000+
**Limitations:** Still limited scale (typically 100-10,000 units)

**Example:** Pilot production of 500 backpacks for direct-to-consumer launch.

### 1.6 Tier 6: Mass Production / Scale

**Purpose:** Full commercial production.

**Materials:**
- Production materials at scale
- Production methods optimized
- Production packaging
- Retail-ready product

**What it tests:**
- Scalability
- Cost efficiency
- Quality consistency
- Supply chain reliability
- Market saturation

**Timeline:** Years
**Cost:** $500,000-$10M+

**Example:** Mass production of 50,000+ backpacks for retail distribution.

### 1.7 Prototyping Tier Summary

| Tier | Name | Purpose | Timeline | Cost | Quantity | What it validates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concept | Form, function | Hours-days | $0-50 | 1 | Silhouette, basic fit |
| 2 | Muslin | Fit, pattern | Days-weeks | $50-500 | 1-3 | Pattern accuracy, construction |
| 3 | Sample | Materials, function | Weeks-months | $500-5K | 1-10 | Material behavior, function |
| 4 | PP Sample | Production readiness | Months | $5K-50K | 10-100 | Manufacturing process |
| 5 | Pilot | Market response | Months-years | $50K-500K | 100-10K | Market demand, business model |
| 6 | Mass Production | Scale | Years | $500K-10M+ | 10K+ | Scalability, cost efficiency |

## 2. Construction Techniques

### 2.1 Cut-and-Sew

**What:** Traditional garment construction — fabric is cut into pieces and sewn together.

**Process:**
1. Pattern creation (flat pattern or draping)
2. Fabric cutting (manual or CNC)
3. Piece preparation (notching, marking, bundling)
4. Sewing (lockstitch, overlock, coverstitch)
5. Finishing (pressing, topstitching, hemming)
6. Quality control


### 2.2 Bonding

**What:** Layers of fabric bonded together with adhesive or heat instead of stitching.

**Types:**
- **Adhesive bonding:** Fabric + adhesive film + fabric
- **Heat bonding:** Thermoplastic film activated by heat
- **Ultrasonic welding:** High-frequency vibration bonds thermoplastics

**Applications:**
- Seamless garments (no visible seams)
- Technical outerwear (waterproof-breathable)
- Laminated fabrics
- Sportswear
- Medical textiles


### 2.3 Welding

**What:** Ultrasonic or radio-frequency welding of thermoplastic materials.

**Types:**
- **Ultrasonic welding:** High-frequency vibration melts and bonds
- **Radio-frequency welding:** RF energy melts and bonds
- **Hot-air welding:** Hot air melts and bonds

**Applications:**
- Inflatable products (rafts, air mattresses)
- Technical outerwear (seamless seams)
- Medical devices (IV bags, blood bags)
- Automotive (airbags, interior)


### 2.4 Knitting (Cut-and-Knit)

**What:** Fabric is knitted in panels, then cut and sewn together.

**Types:**
- **Warp knitting:** Yarn runs lengthwise, interlooped
- **Weft knitting:** Yarn runs across, interlooped
- **Circular knitting:** Continuous tube knitting
- **Flat knitting:** Panel knitting

**Applications:**
- Knitwear (sweaters, cardigans)
- Sportswear (compression, activewear)
- Socks, underwear
- Technical textiles


### 2.5 3D Knitting (Whole-Garment)

**What:** Entire garment knitted in one piece, no cutting or sewing.

**Types:**
- **Whole-garment knitting:** Complete garment in one piece
- **Shaped knitting:** Garment shape knitted, minimal cutting
- **Intarsia:** Multi-color patterns knitted
- **Jacquard:** Complex patterns knitted

**Applications:**
- Sportswear (Nike Flyknit, Adidas Primeknit)
- Socks (Stance, Bombas)
- Underwear (Lululemon, Aerie)
- Technical textiles


### 2.6 Laser Cutting

**What:** Fabric or material cut with laser precision.

**Types:**
- **CO2 laser:** For organic materials (fabric, leather, paper)
- **Fiber laser:** For metals, plastics
- **UV laser:** For delicate materials

**Applications:**
- Intricate patterns (lace, filigree)
- Precision cutting (technical textiles)
- Engraving (leather, fabric)
- Edging (seal fabric edges)
- Prototyping (rapid iteration)


### 2.7 3D Printing

**What:** Additive manufacturing of garments or components.

**Types:**
- **FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling):** Plastic filament
- **SLS (Selective Laser Sintering):** Powdered plastic/metal
- **SLA (Stereolithography):** Resin
- **DLP (Digital Light Processing):** Resin, faster than SLA
- **MJF (Multi Jet Fusion):** Nylon powder

**Applications:**
- Custom-fit shoes (midsole, upper)
- Orthotics and insoles
- Fashion accessories (jewelry, bags)
- Prototyping (rapid iteration)
- Experimental fashion


### 2.8 Construction Technique Comparison

| Technique | Complexity | Speed | Cost | Versatility | Quality | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cut-and-sew | Low | Medium | Low | High | Good | Most garments, small batches |
| Bonding | Medium | High | Medium | Medium | Excellent | Technical outerwear, seamless |
| Welding | High | High | High | Low | Excellent | Inflatable, technical, medical |
| Knitting | Medium | High | Medium | Medium | Good | Knitwear, sportswear |
| 3D knitting | High | Medium | High | Low | Excellent | Sportswear, seamless, sustainable |
| Laser cutting | Medium | High | Medium | Medium | Excellent | Intricate designs, technical |
| 3D printing | High | Low | High | Low | Good | Custom-fit, prototyping, experimental |

### 3.2 Hardware Quality Checklist

| Factor | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Material** | Metal type (zinc alloy, brass, stainless steel), plastic type (ABS, nylon) |
| **Finish** | Plating, coating, durability, corrosion resistance |
| **Strength** | Load capacity, wear resistance, cycle testing |
| **Function** | Smooth operation, reliability, ease of use |

| **Hybrid** | Combine standard + custom | Balance cost and uniqueness |
| **White label** | Supplier's product with your branding | Fast market entry, lower cost |
| **Private label** | Exclusive product for you | Control, differentiation |

## 4. Production Models


### 4.2 Production Models

| Model | Description | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **In-house** | Own factory | Control, quality, IP | High cost, complexity | Large brands, luxury |
| **Contract manufacturing** | Hire factory | Lower cost, expertise | Less control | Most brands |
| **CMT (Cut-Make-Trim)** | Factory does all | Simple, one-stop | Less control | Small-medium brands |
| **CMT + sourcing** | Factory + separate sourcing | Flexibility | Coordination | Medium brands |
| **Private label** | Buy existing product, rebrand | Fast, low risk | Less differentiation | Market entry, testing |
| **White label** | Buy existing product, add branding | Fast, low cost | Minimal differentiation | Budget brands |
| **Print-on-demand** | Print as ordered | No inventory, customization | Higher unit cost | Niche, personalized |
| **Made-to-order** | Produce after order | No inventory, customization | Longer lead time | Luxury, custom |
| **Crowdfunding** | Fund production via campaign | Validation, funding | Risk, delays | Startups, new products |
| **Dropshipping** | Supplier ships directly | No inventory | Less control | E-commerce |

### 4.3 Production Decision Framework

| Question | If yes → | If no → |
|---|---|---|
| Do you have $100K+ budget? | Contract manufacturing | In-house or CMT |
| Do you need 10K+ units? | Mass production | Small-batch |
| Do you need customization? | Made-to-order | Standard production |
| Do you need fast market entry? | Private label | Custom production |
| Do you need IP protection? | In-house or controlled contract | Standard contract |
| Do you need sustainability? | Certified suppliers | Standard suppliers |
| Do you need quality control? | In-house or tight contract | Standard contract |

## 5. Quality Control Checkpoints

### 5.1 QC Checkpoints

| Stage | What to check | Who checks |
|---|---|---|
| **Material receipt** | Fabric quality, color, defects | QC inspector |
| **Pattern approval** | Pattern accuracy, fit | Pattern maker |
| **Sample approval** | Construction, fit, function | Designer, QC |
| **Pre-production** | Production readiness, materials | Production manager |
| **In-line** | Stitching, seams, construction | Line QC |
| **Final inspection** | Overall quality, defects | Final QC |
| **Pre-shipment** | Packaging, labeling, quantity | Shipping QC |
| **Post-delivery** | Customer feedback, returns | Customer service |

### 5.2 QC Metrics

| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| **Defect rate** | <2% | Defects per unit |
| **Stitch defects** | <1% | Skipped stitches, loose threads |
| **Seam defects** | <1% | Open seams, puckering |
| **Hardware defects** | <0.5% | Broken, missing, misaligned |
| **Color variation** | Within tolerance | Color matching |
| **Size accuracy** | Within tolerance | Measurement checks |
| **Functionality** | 100% | Function tests |
| **Safety** | 100% | Safety tests |

### 5.3 QC Methods

| Method | Description | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual inspection** | Look for defects | All stages |
| **Measurement** | Check dimensions | Pattern, sample, production |
| **Function test** | Test functionality | Sample, production |
| **Durability test** | Test wear and tear | Sample, pre-production |
| **Safety test** | Test safety standards | Pre-production, production |
| **AQL inspection** | Acceptable Quality Limit | Production, pre-shipment |
| **Statistical sampling** | Sample-based inspection | Production |
| **100% inspection** | Inspect every unit | High-value, safety-critical |

## 6. Cost Driver Analysis

### 6.1 Cost Components

| Component | % of total | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| **Materials** | 30-50% | Fabric, trims, hardware, packaging |
| **Labor** | 20-40% | Sewing, assembly, finishing |
| **Overhead** | 10-20% | Factory rent, utilities, equipment |
| **Marketing** | 10-30% | Advertising, promotion, photography |
| **Logistics** | 5-15% | Shipping, warehousing, fulfillment |
| **Returns** | 2-10% | Returns, exchanges, refunds |
| **Other** | 5-10% | Duties, taxes, insurance, legal |

### 6.2 Cost Driver Framework

| Driver | Impact | How to optimize |
|---|---|---|
| **Quantity** | Higher quantity = lower unit cost | Order larger quantities |
| **Complexity** | More complex = higher cost | Simplify design |
| **Materials** | Premium materials = higher cost | Choose cost-effective alternatives |
| **Labor** | Labor-intensive = higher cost | Automate, simplify construction |
| **Location** | Labor cost varies by region | Choose cost-effective manufacturing location |
| **Lead time** | Rush orders = higher cost | Plan ahead, avoid rush |
| **Customization** | Custom = higher cost | Standardize where possible |
| **Quality** | Higher quality = higher cost | Balance quality and cost |
| **Sustainability** | Sustainable = higher cost | Choose cost-effective sustainable options |

### 6.3 Cost Calculation Example

**Product:** Backpack
**Quantity:** 1,000 units

| Cost item | Unit cost | Total cost |
|---|---|---|
| Materials (fabric, hardware, trims) | $15.00 | $15,000 |
| Labor (cutting, sewing, assembly) | $8.00 | $8,000 |
| Overhead | $3.00 | $3,000 |
| Packaging | $2.00 | $2,000 |
| Shipping (to warehouse) | $2.00 | $2,000 |
| **Total cost** | **$30.00** | **$30,000** |

**Retail price:** $60.00 (100% markup)
**Gross margin:** $30.00 (50%)

**Cost optimization opportunities:**
- Increase quantity to 5,000 → unit cost drops to $25.00
- Simplify design (fewer pockets) → labor cost drops to $6.00
- Choose alternative fabric → material cost drops to $12.00
- Negotiate better rates → overhead drops to $2.50

**Result:** Unit cost drops to $22.50, gross margin increases to $37.50 (62.5%)

## 7. Sustainable Manufacturing Options

### 7.1 Sustainable Materials

| Material | Sustainability benefit | Cost impact | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recycled polyester** | Reduces plastic waste | +10-20% | Widely available |
| **Recycled nylon** | Reduces ocean waste | +15-25% | Widely available |
| **Organic cotton** | No pesticides, less water | +20-40% | Widely available |
| **Hemp** | Low water, no pesticides | +10-20% | Growing availability |
| **Tencel/Lyocell** | Closed-loop, biodegradable | +15-30% | Widely available |
| **Mycelium leather** | Animal-free, biodegradable | +50-100% | Limited availability |
| **Piñatex** | Pineapple waste, sustainable | +30-50% | Limited availability |
| **Recycled paper** | Recycled content | +5-10% | Widely available |
| **Bio-based plastics** | Renewable, biodegradable | +20-50% | Growing availability |

### 7.2 Sustainable Manufacturing Processes

| Process | Sustainability benefit | Cost impact | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3D knitting** | No cutting waste, less energy | +20-40% | High |
| **Digital printing** | Less water, less waste | +10-20% | Medium |
| **Laser cutting** | No cutting waste, sealed edges | +15-30% | Medium |
| **Water-based inks** | Less toxic | +10-20% | Low |
| **Natural dyes** | Biodegradable, less toxic | +20-50% | Medium |
| **Solar power** | Renewable energy | -5-15% | Low |
| **Local production** | Less transport emissions | +10-30% | Low |
| **Zero-waste patterning** | No fabric waste | +5-15% | Medium |
| **Modular design** | Repairable, replaceable | +10-20% | Medium |
| **Circular design** | Recyclable, biodegradable | +10-30% | High |

### 7.3 Sustainable Manufacturing Checklist

- [ ] Materials: Recycled, organic, bio-based, or certified sustainable
- [ ] Process: Energy-efficient, water-efficient, low-waste
- [ ] Labor: Fair wages, safe conditions, ethical sourcing
- [ ] Packaging: Recycled, minimal, biodegradable
- [ ] Logistics: Local production, efficient shipping
- [ ] End-of-life: Recyclable, biodegradable, take-back program
- [ ] Certifications: GOTS, OEKO-TEX, Fair Trade, B Corp
- [ ] Transparency: Supply chain visibility, reporting

## 8. Complexity Ratings

| Rating | Description | Cost impact | Timeline impact | Skill requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1/5** | Simple, basic construction | Low | Short | Low skill |
