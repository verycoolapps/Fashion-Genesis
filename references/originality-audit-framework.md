# Originality Audit Framework

Reference module for Fashion Genesis X. This module provides a rigorous, honest framework for evaluating the originality of fashion concepts, designs, and inventions. It covers prior-art search strategy, similarity mapping, design-around thinking, IP protection routes, freedom-to-operate considerations, and how to write originality assessments that don't overclaim.

Status convention: **[VERIFIED]** = established practice, **[EXPERIMENTAL]** = emerging method, **[SPECULATIVE]** = unproven approach.

---

## 1. The Originality Problem in Fashion

Fashion is unique among creative fields because:

- **Inspiration is expected** — designers reference history, culture, other designers.
- **Trends cycle** — what was novel becomes standard within seasons.
- **IP protection is limited** — most fashion is not patentable; design patents cover only ornamental features.
- **Copying is rampant** — fast fashion replicates designs within weeks.
- **Cultural appropriation** — drawing from cultures without permission or compensation.
- **Derivative works** — most "original" designs are recombinations of existing elements.

**The core question:** When is a design "original" vs. "derivative" vs. "copy"?

**Answer:** There's no bright line. Originality exists on a spectrum. The audit framework helps locate a concept on that spectrum honestly.

## 2. Prior-Art Search Strategy

### 2.1 Search Scope

A comprehensive prior-art search covers:

| Source | What it covers | Access |
|---|---|---|
| **Patents** | Utility patents, design patents | Google Patents, USPTO, Espacenet, WIPO |
| **Trademarks** | Brand names, logos, slogans | USPTO TESS, EUIPO, WIPO Madrid |
| **Copyrights** | Artistic works, prints, patterns | US Copyright Office, Copyright.eu |
| **Products** | Commercial products, retail | Google Shopping, Amazon, brand websites |
| **Runway archives** | Fashion shows, collections | Vogue Runway, Style.com, brand archives |
| **Social media** | Influencer designs, viral products | Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest |
| **Academic literature** | Research papers, theses | Google Scholar, JSTOR, university repositories |
| **Trade publications** | Industry magazines, reports | Women's Wear Daily, BoF, Sourcing Journal |
| **Conference proceedings** | Innovation conferences, trade shows | Textile Exchange, Première Vision, Pitti |
| **Crowdfunding** | Kickstarter, Indiegogo campaigns | Kickstarter, Indiegogo |
| **Patents pending** | Published patent applications | Google Patents, USPTO |
| **Expired patents** | Public domain inventions | Google Patents, USPTO |
| **Defensive publications** | Publications to prevent patenting | IP.com, Research Disclosure |
| **Non-patent literature** | Technical papers, standards | IEEE, ISO, ASTM |

### 2.2 Search Methodology

**Step 1: Define the search scope**

- **Keywords:** Technical terms, material names, function descriptions.
- **Classes/codes:** IPC/CPC codes for patents, NICE classes for trademarks.
- **Date range:** Last 5-10 years for fashion, all time for fundamentals.
- **Geography:** Global, regional, or specific markets.
- **Language:** English, plus relevant languages (French, Italian, Japanese, Korean).

**Step 2: Execute systematic searches**

| Database | Search method | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Patents** | Keyword + CPC/IPC codes | Use "patent", "design patent", "trademark" filters |
| **USPTO** | TESS for trademarks, Patent Center for patents | Use image search for design patents |
| **Espacenet** | Advanced search, classification | Global coverage, machine translation |
| **WIPO** | Global brand database, PCT | International coverage |
| **Google Scholar** | Academic papers | Find research that may become patents |
| **Amazon/retail** | Product search | Find commercial products |
| **Runway archives** | Designer/collection search | Find fashion precedents |

**Step 3: Analyze results**

For each relevant result:
- **Document type:** Patent, trademark, product, publication, etc.
- **Date:** When was it created/published?
- **Status:** Active, expired, abandoned, pending?
- **Claims:** What does it cover? (for patents)
- **Similarity:** How similar to your concept?
- **Relevance:** Does it block your concept or just inform it?

**Step 4: Document findings**

Create a prior-art log:

| # | Source | Type | Date | Title | Relevance | Similarity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | US Patent 10,123,456 | Utility patent | 2019 | Smart jacket with sensors | High | Medium | Covers sensor integration |
| 2 | Nike Flyknit | Product | 2012 | Knit upper running shoe | High | High | Knit upper construction |
| 3 | Alexander McQueen runway | Runway | 2015 | 3D-printed dress | Medium | Low | Aesthetic inspiration only |

### 2.3 Search Limitations

**What prior-art search CANNOT do:**

- ❌ Guarantee no infringement exists (searches are imperfect)
- ❌ Cover all languages and databases
- ❌ Find unpublished work in progress
- ❌ Predict future developments
- ❌ Determine legal outcomes (only courts can)

**What it CAN do:**

- ✅ Identify known prior art
- ✅ Inform design-around decisions
- ✅ Assess freedom-to-operate risk
- ✅ Strengthen IP position
- ✅ Guide patent strategy

### 2.4 When Prior-Art Search Is Unavailable

**Label as PRELIMINARY:**

If you cannot conduct a thorough prior-art search (no access to databases, time constraints, specialized field), label the originality assessment as **PRELIMINARY** and note:

- What was searched
- What was not searched
- What assumptions were made
- What further research is needed

**Example:**

> **PRELIMINARY ORIGINALITY ASSESSMENT**
> Based on limited search of Google Patents and USPTO (keywords: "modular backpack," "convertible garment"), no directly conflicting patents found. However, comprehensive search of international databases, academic literature, and product catalogs was not conducted. This assessment should be verified before commercial launch.

## 3. Similarity Mapping

### 3.1 Similarity Dimensions

| Dimension | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional similarity** | Does it do the same thing? | 30% |
| **Structural similarity** | Is it built the same way? | 25% |
| **Aesthetic similarity** | Does it look the same? | 20% |
| **Material similarity** | Is it made of the same stuff? | 15% |
| **Experience similarity** | Does it feel the same to use? | 10% |

### 3.2 Similarity Scoring

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **0-2** | No meaningful similarity | Original, proceed |
| **3-4** | Minor similarity | Likely original, minor design-around |
| **5-6** | Moderate similarity | Derivative, needs differentiation |
| **7-8** | High similarity | Copy or close derivative, redesign |
| **9-10** | Near-identical | Direct copy, significant legal risk |

### 3.3 Similarity Mapping Process

1. **Select reference points** — existing products/patents that are similar.
2. **Score each dimension** — rate similarity 0-10 for each dimension.
3. **Calculate weighted score** — apply weights to get overall similarity.
4. **Identify differentiators** — what makes your concept different?
5. **Assess risk** — does similarity create legal or market risk?

**Example: Modular backpack concept**

| Reference | Functional | Structural | Aesthetic | Material | Experience | Weighted Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak Design backpack | 8 | 7 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 7.0 |
| Osprey backpack | 7 | 6 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 6.6 |
| Military MOLLE pack | 6 | 8 | 3 | 7 | 6 | 6.1 |
| Your concept | — | — | — | — | — | — |

**Analysis:** Your concept is moderately similar to existing backpacks. Differentiation needed in at least 2 dimensions to achieve originality score <5.

## 4. Design-Around Thinking

### 4.1 What is Design-Around?

Design-around is the process of creating a new design that achieves the same function as an existing patented design but uses different mechanisms, materials, or structures to avoid infringement.

**Example:**

- **Patent:** Nike's Flyknit upper construction (specific knitting pattern)
- **Design-around:** Different knitting pattern, different yarn, different structure that achieves similar result

### 4.2 Design-Around Strategies

| Strategy | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Change mechanism** | Use different mechanism for same function | Magnetic closure instead of zipper |
| **Change material** | Use different material | Recycled polyester instead of nylon |
| **Change structure** | Use different structure | 3D-printed instead of knit |
| **Change process** | Use different manufacturing | Digital knitting instead of cut-and-sew |
| **Change scale** | Different size/scale | Mini version, oversized version |
| **Change configuration** | Different arrangement | Modular instead of fixed |
| **Change interaction** | Different user interaction | One-handed instead of two-handed |
| **Change context** | Different use case | Urban instead of outdoor |
| **Combine elements** | Combine from different sources | Hybrid of two existing designs |
| **Invert elements** | Reverse existing design | Inside-out, reversible |

### 4.3 Design-Around Process

1. **Identify the blocking patent/design** — what exactly does it cover?
2. **Analyze the claims** — what are the specific elements that are protected?
3. **Find alternative mechanisms** — what else could achieve the same function?
4. **Evaluate alternatives** — do they avoid infringement? Are they viable?
5. **Document the design-around** — keep records of your design process.
6. **Seek legal review** — have a patent attorney review before launch.

### 4.4 Design-Around Limitations

**When design-around is NOT possible:**

- The function is inherently tied to the mechanism (e.g., specific chemical process)
- All alternatives are covered by other patents
- The design is so fundamental that any implementation infringes
- The patent covers the concept, not just the implementation

**When design-around IS possible:**

- Multiple mechanisms can achieve the same function
- Different materials can be used
- Different structures can be built
- Different processes can be employed

## 5. IP Protection Routes for Fashion

### 5.1 Types of IP Protection

| Type | What it protects | Duration | Cost | Fashion applicability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Utility patent** | Inventions, processes, compositions | 20 years | $10K-$50K+ | Smart textiles, manufacturing processes, functional innovations |
| **Design patent** | Ornamental design of functional item | 15 years | $1K-$5K | Product design, packaging, UI |
| **Trademark** | Brand names, logos, slogans | Indefinite (with renewal) | $250-$1K | Brand identity, product names |
| **Trade dress** | Product appearance, packaging | Indefinite (with use) | Varies | Product design, store design |
| **Copyright** | Artistic works, prints, patterns | Life + 70 years | $45-$65 | Prints, patterns, artistic elements |
| **Trade secret** | Confidential business information | Indefinite (if kept secret) | Minimal | Manufacturing processes, formulas |

### 5.2 Fashion-Specific IP Considerations

**What CAN be protected:**

- ✅ Unique textile prints and patterns (copyright)
- ✅ Innovative manufacturing processes (utility patent)
- ✅ Smart textile technologies (utility patent)
- ✅ Product designs that are ornamental (design patent)
- ✅ Brand names and logos (trademark)
- ✅ Product appearance that identifies source (trade dress)
- ✅ Artistic elements (copyright)

**What CANNOT be protected:**

- ❌ Basic garment shapes (dress, shirt, pants)
- ❌ Standard construction techniques (seams, darts, pleats)
- ❌ Common materials (cotton, polyester, denim)
- ❌ General style trends (oversized, cropped, etc.)
- ❌ Functional features that are necessary (pockets, zippers)
- ❌ Ideas and concepts (only implementations)

### 5.3 IP Strategy for Fashion Concepts

| Concept type | Recommended IP route | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Smart textile** | Utility patent | Functional innovation, technical |
| **Manufacturing process** | Utility patent | Process innovation |
| **Product design** | Design patent | Ornamental features |
| **Brand name** | Trademark | Brand identity |
| **Print/pattern** | Copyright | Artistic work |
| **Product appearance** | Trade dress | Source identification |
| **Formula/recipe** | Trade secret | Confidential information |
| **Software** | Copyright, patent | Digital innovation |

### 5.4 IP Timeline Considerations

| Stage | IP action | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Concept** | Document invention, prior-art search | Before public disclosure |
| **Prototype** | File provisional patent (if applicable) | Before public disclosure |
| **Pre-launch** | File non-provisional patent, trademark | Before commercial launch |
| **Launch** | Monitor for infringement | Ongoing |
| **Post-launch** | Enforce IP rights | As needed |

**Critical rule:** Public disclosure before filing can destroy patent rights. File provisional patents early if innovation is valuable.

## 6. Freedom-to-Operate (FTO) Considerations

### 6.1 What is FTO?

Freedom-to-operate is the ability to make, use, or sell a product without infringing existing IP rights. FTO analysis is essential before commercial launch.

### 6.2 FTO Analysis Process

1. **Identify relevant IP** — patents, trademarks, copyrights that could apply.
2. **Analyze claims** — what exactly do they cover?
3. **Compare to your design** — does your design infringe?
4. **Assess risk** — how likely is infringement? What's the consequence?
5. **Develop strategy** — design-around, license, or avoid.

### 6.3 FTO Risk Factors

| Risk factor | Description | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Patent coverage** | Broad patent claims | Design-around, license |
| **Trademark similarity** | Confusingly similar marks | Choose different name |
| **Copyright similarity** | Similar artistic elements | Different design |
| **Trade dress** | Similar product appearance | Different appearance |
| **Geographic scope** | Patent valid in some countries only | Launch in non-covered markets |
| **Time remaining** | Patent expiring soon | Wait for expiration |
| **Enforcement likelihood** | Patent holder aggressive vs. passive | Assess enforcement history |

### 6.4 FTO Documentation

**Create an FTO memo:**

```
FREEDOM-TO-OPERATE ANALYSIS
Product: [Name]
Date: [Date]
Analyst: [Name]

1. RELEVANT IP IDENTIFIED
- Patent US1234567: [Description]
- Trademark "BrandX": [Description]
- Copyright registration: [Description]

2. ANALYSIS
- Patent US1234567: Your design uses [different mechanism], does not infringe claims 1-5
- Trademark "BrandX": Your name "BrandY" is sufficiently different
- Copyright: Your print is original, does not copy protected work

3. RISK ASSESSMENT
- Patent risk: LOW (design-around successful)
- Trademark risk: LOW (sufficiently different)
- Copyright risk: LOW (original work)

4. RECOMMENDATION
- Proceed with launch
- Monitor for new IP filings
- Consider defensive publication
```

## 7. Writing an Originality Assessment

### 7.1 Assessment Structure

```
ORIGINALITY ASSESSMENT
Concept: [Name/Description]
Date: [Date]
Assessor: [Name]
Status: [FINAL / PRELIMINARY]

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
[2-3 sentence summary of originality findings]

2. SEARCH SCOPE
- Databases searched: [List]
- Keywords used: [List]
- Date range: [Range]
- Geography: [Scope]
- Limitations: [What was not searched]

3. PRIOR-ART FINDINGS
[Table of relevant prior art with relevance scores]

4. SIMILARITY ANALYSIS
[Similarity mapping results]

5. DIFFERENTIATORS
[What makes this concept original]

6. RISK ASSESSMENT
- IP risk: [LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH]
- Market risk: [LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH]
- Legal risk: [LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH]

7. RECOMMENDATIONS
- Proceed / Revise / Discard
- Design-around opportunities
- IP protection strategy
- Further research needed

8. DISCLAIMERS
This assessment is based on [limited/comprehensive] search. It does not constitute legal advice. Consult a qualified patent attorney before commercial launch.
```

### 7.2 Avoiding Overclaiming

**DO:**

- ✅ State what was searched and what wasn't
- ✅ Use cautious language ("appears original," "no conflicting patents found")
- ✅ Acknowledge limitations and uncertainties
- ✅ Recommend further research
- ✅ Label as PRELIMINARY when appropriate
- ✅ Cite specific sources
- ✅ Distinguish between "no prior art found" and "no prior art exists"

**DON'T:**

- ❌ Claim "this is completely original" without evidence
- ❌ Say "no one has done this before" without exhaustive search
- ❌ Ignore potential prior art in other languages/geographies
- ❌ Claim legal conclusions (only courts can)
- ❌ Present PRELIMINARY assessment as FINAL
- ❌ Omit limitations and uncertainties

### 7.3 Example Assessment

```
ORIGINALITY ASSESSMENT
Concept: Modular Backpack with Integrated Solar Charging
Date: 2024-01-15
Assessor: Fashion Genesis X
Status: PRELIMINARY

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The concept combines modular backpack design with integrated solar charging. While modular backpacks and solar-powered backpacks exist separately, the specific combination appears to have limited prior art. However, comprehensive search was not conducted across all databases and languages.

2. SEARCH SCOPE
- Databases searched: Google Patents, USPTO, Google Shopping, Kickstarter
- Keywords used: "modular backpack," "solar backpack," "convertible backpack," "charging backpack"
- Date range: 2015-2024
- Geography: US, EU
- Limitations: No search of Asian databases, academic literature, or non-English sources

3. PRIOR-ART FINDINGS
| # | Source | Type | Date | Relevance | Similarity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nomad Lifeline | Product | 2018 | High | Medium |
| 2 | Voltaic Systems | Product | 2019 | High | Medium |
| 3 | Peak Design backpack | Product | 2020 | Medium | Low |
| 4 | US Patent 10,987,654 | Patent | 2021 | Medium | Low |

4. SIMILARITY ANALYSIS
- Functional similarity: 7/10 (solar charging + modular storage)
- Structural similarity: 5/10 (different modular system)
- Aesthetic similarity: 4/10 (different design language)
- Material similarity: 6/10 (similar materials)
- Experience similarity: 6/10 (similar user experience)
- Weighted score: 5.8/10 (moderate similarity)

5. DIFFERENTIATORS
- Unique modular attachment system (quick-release, tool-free)
- Integrated solar panel with higher efficiency
- Modular components (detachable solar panel, battery pack)
- Sustainable materials (recycled ocean plastic)

6. RISK ASSESSMENT
- IP risk: MEDIUM (some patents may cover aspects)
- Market risk: LOW (growing demand for sustainable tech)
- Legal risk: LOW (no obvious infringement)

7. RECOMMENDATIONS
- Proceed with development, but conduct comprehensive FTO search before launch
- Consider design-around for modular attachment system
- File provisional patent for unique modular system
- Conduct user testing to validate market demand

8. DISCLAIMERS
This is a PRELIMINARY assessment based on limited search. It does not constitute legal advice. Consult a qualified patent attorney before commercial launch.
```
