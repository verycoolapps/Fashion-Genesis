# Fashion Business Intelligence

Reference module for Fashion Genesis X. Covers market sizing, positioning, pricing architecture, customer personas, go-to-market paths, IP strategy, crowdfunding/prototyping budgets, sustainability claims, and competitive analysis.

Status convention: **[VERIFIED]** = established practice, **[EXPERIMENTAL]** = emerging method, **[SPECULATIVE]** = unproven approach.

## 1. Market Sizing Approach

### 1.1 Market Sizing Methods

| Method | Description | When to use | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top-down** | Start with total market, segment down | Initial estimates | Low |
| **Bottom-up** | Start with target customers, build up | Detailed estimates | High |
| **Comparable analysis** | Use similar products/markets | Validation | Medium |
| **Analogous estimation** | Use similar past launches | Validation | Medium |
| **Data-driven** | Use actual sales/market data | Mature markets | High |

### 1.2 Top-Down Market Sizing

**Process:**
1. Identify total addressable market (TAM)
2. Identify serviceable addressable market (SAM)
3. Identify serviceable obtainable market (SOM)

**Example: Sustainable backpack market**

| Level | Definition | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| TAM | Total backpack market | $10B | $10B |
| SAM | Sustainable backpack market | 20% of TAM | $2B |
| SOM | Obtainable market share | 1% of SAM | $20M |

**Limitations:**
- Assumes market data is accurate
- Doesn't account for new market creation
- May overestimate or underestimate

### 1.3 Bottom-Up Market Sizing

**Process:**
1. Define target customer segment
2. Estimate number of potential customers
3. Estimate purchase frequency
4. Estimate average order value
5. Calculate market size

**Example: Sustainable backpack market**

| Factor | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Target customers | 10M eco-conscious consumers | 10M |
| Purchase frequency | 1 backpack per 3 years | 0.33/year |
| Average order value | $80 | $80 |
| Market size | 10M × 0.33 × $80 | $264M |

**Advantages:**
- More accurate for niche markets
- Accounts for customer behavior
- Can be validated with customer data

**Limitations:**
- Requires customer research
- Assumes customer behavior
- May underestimate market expansion

### 1.4 Market Sizing Data Sources

| Source | Data type | Access | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Industry reports** | Market size, growth, trends | Paid reports | $500-$5K+ |
| **Government data** | Census, economic data | Free | Free |
| **Trade associations** | Industry statistics | Membership | $100-$1K/year |
| **Company reports** | Competitor data | Public filings | Free |
| **Customer surveys** | Customer behavior | Custom research | $5K-$50K |
| **Social media** | Social trends | Free tools | Free |
| **Search data** | Search trends | Google Trends | Free |
| **Sales data** | Actual sales | Internal data | Internal |

### 1.5 Market Sizing Checklist

- [ ] Define target market clearly
- [ ] Choose appropriate method (top-down vs. bottom-up)
- [ ] Gather relevant data from multiple sources
- [ ] Validate assumptions with customer research
- [ ] Account for market growth/decline
- [ ] Consider competitive landscape
- [ ] Document methodology and assumptions
- [ ] Update regularly as market evolves

## 3. Pricing Architecture

### 3.1 Pricing Methods

| Method | Description | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost-plus** | Cost + markup | Standard products | $30 cost → $60 price (100% markup) |
| **Value-based** | Based on perceived value | Differentiated products | $60 price for $100 perceived value |
| **Competitive** | Based on competitors | Competitive markets | Match competitor pricing |
| **Penetration** | Low price to gain share | New market entry | $30 price to gain share |
| **Skimming** | High price to maximize profit | Premium products | $100 price for early adopters |
| **Dynamic** | Adjust based on demand | Variable demand | Higher price during peak |
| **Freemium** | Free + paid features | Digital products | Free basic, paid premium |

### 3.2 Pricing Strategy Framework

**Step 1: Calculate costs**
- Materials cost
- Labor cost
- Overhead cost
- Marketing cost
- Logistics cost
- Returns cost
- Total cost per unit

**Step 2: Determine target margin**
- Gross margin target (typically 50-70%)
- Net margin target (typically 10-20%)

**Step 3: Choose pricing method**
- Cost-plus: Cost × (1 + markup)
- Value-based: Perceived value × (1 - discount)
- Competitive: Competitor price ± adjustment

**Step 4: Validate pricing**
- Customer willingness to pay
- Competitive positioning
- Market acceptance
- Profitability analysis

### 3.3 Pricing Example

**Product:** Sustainable backpack
**Cost breakdown:**

| Cost item | Unit cost |
|---|---|
| Materials | $15.00 |
| Labor | $8.00 |
| Overhead | $3.00 |
| Packaging | $2.00 |
| Shipping | $2.00 |
| **Total cost** | **$30.00** |

**Pricing options:**

| Method | Calculation | Price | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus (100%) | $30 × 2 | $60.00 | $30.00 (50%) |
| Cost-plus (150%) | $30 × 2.5 | $75.00 | $45.00 (60%) |
| Value-based | $100 perceived × 0.7 | $70.00 | $40.00 (57%) |
| Competitive | Competitor $65 ± $5 | $60.00 | $30.00 (50%) |

**Recommendation:** Price at $65-70 for premium positioning, $55-60 for mid-range.

### 3.4 Pricing Checklist

- [ ] Costs calculated accurately
- [ ] Target margin defined
- [ ] Pricing method chosen
- [ ] Competitive pricing analyzed
- [ ] Customer willingness to pay validated
- [ ] Pricing tested with customers
- [ ] Profitability analyzed
- [ ] Pricing strategy documented

## 4. Customer Personas

### 4.1 Persona Framework

**Persona components:**
- **Name:** Fictional name
- **Age:** Age range
- **Occupation:** Job/role
- **Income:** Income range
- **Location:** Geographic location
- **Lifestyle:** Daily life, interests
- **Goals:** What they want to achieve
- **Pain points:** What problems they face
- **Buying behavior:** How they shop
- **Values:** What they care about
- **Media consumption:** Where they get information

### 4.2 Example Persona: Eco-Conscious Commuter

**Name:** Sarah
**Age:** 28-35
**Occupation:** Marketing manager
**Income:** $60K-80K
**Location:** Urban, US
**Lifestyle:** Commutes by bike/public transit, values sustainability, active lifestyle
**Goals:** Reduce environmental impact, stay active, look good
**Pain points:** Limited storage, durability concerns, high prices for sustainable options
**Buying behavior:** Researches online, reads reviews, values transparency
**Values:** Sustainability, quality, ethics, convenience
**Media consumption:** Instagram, blogs, podcasts, sustainable fashion sites

**Implications for product:**
- Sustainable materials (recycled, organic)
- Durable construction
- Compact, functional design
- Transparent pricing
- Ethical manufacturing
- Easy care

### 4.3 Persona Development Process

1. **Define target market** — who are you trying to reach?
2. **Gather data** — customer surveys, interviews, analytics
3. **Identify segments** — group customers by characteristics
4. **Create personas** — fictional representations of segments
5. **Validate personas** — test with real customers
6. **Use personas** — guide product, marketing, design decisions

### 4.4 Persona Checklist

- [ ] Target market defined

- [ ] Segments identified
- [ ] Personas created
- [ ] Personas validated with customers

## 5. Go-to-Market Paths

### 5.1 GTM Paths

| Path | Description | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct-to-consumer (DTC)** | Sell directly to customers | Higher margin, customer data, brand control | Marketing cost, logistics | Brands with strong brand, e-commerce |
| **Wholesale** | Sell to retailers | Volume, retail presence, lower marketing | Lower margin, less control | Brands with retail relationships |
| **Marketplaces** | Sell on Amazon, Etsy, etc. | Large audience, easy setup | Competition, fees, less control | New brands, testing |
| **Collaborations** | Partner with other brands | Shared audience, credibility | Revenue sharing, less control | Brands seeking exposure |
| **Crowdfunding** | Fund via Kickstarter, Indiegogo | Validation, funding, community | Risk, delays, fees | New products, startups |
| **Subscription** | Recurring revenue model | Predictable revenue, customer loyalty | Churn, inventory management | Consumables, replenishment |
| **Pop-up** | Temporary retail locations | Low cost, testing, buzz | Limited duration, logistics | Testing, brand awareness |
| **Showroom** | Sales space for buyers | Professional presentation, buyer meetings | Cost, limited reach | Wholesale brands |

### 5.2 GTM Strategy Framework

**Step 1: Define target customer**
- Who are you selling to?
- What are their needs?
- Where do they shop?

**Step 2: Choose GTM path**
- Which path best reaches your customer?
- What are the pros/cons?
- What is the cost?

**Step 3: Develop strategy**
- Pricing strategy
- Distribution strategy
- Marketing strategy
- Sales strategy

**Step 4: Execute and iterate**
- Launch
- Monitor performance
- Gather feedback
- Iterate and improve

### 5.3 GTM Checklist

- [ ] Target customer defined
- [ ] GTM path chosen
- [ ] Pricing strategy developed
- [ ] Distribution strategy developed
- [ ] Marketing strategy developed
- [ ] Sales strategy developed
- [ ] Launch plan created
- [ ] Performance metrics defined
- [ ] Feedback loop established
- [ ] Iteration plan created

## 6. IP Strategy for Fashion

### 6.1 IP Protection for Fashion

| IP type | What it protects | Fashion application | Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trademark** | Brand names, logos, slogans | Brand identity | $250-$1K | 6-12 months |
| **Design patent** | Ornamental design | Product design, packaging | $1K-$5K | 12-18 months |
| **Utility patent** | Inventions, processes | Smart textiles, manufacturing | $10K-$50K+ | 18-36 months |
| **Copyright** | Artistic works, prints | Prints, patterns, artistic elements | $45-$65 | 3-6 months |
| **Trade dress** | Product appearance | Product design, store design | Varies | Ongoing |
| **Trade secret** | Confidential information | Manufacturing processes, formulas | Minimal | Indefinite |

### 6.2 IP Strategy Framework

**Step 1: Identify protectable assets**
- Brand name, logo, slogan → Trademark
- Product design → Design patent
- Manufacturing process → Utility patent
- Print/pattern → Copyright
- Product appearance → Trade dress
- Confidential process → Trade secret

**Step 2: Prioritize IP**
- High value + high risk → File immediately
- High value + low risk → File when budget allows
- Low value + high risk → Consider alternatives
- Low value + low risk → Skip or defer

**Step 3: File and maintain**
- File applications
- Monitor for infringement
- Renew as needed
- Enforce rights

**Step 4: Integrate IP into business**
- IP as competitive advantage
- IP licensing opportunities
- IP as valuation factor

### 6.3 IP Strategy Checklist

- [ ] Protectable assets identified
- [ ] IP prioritized
- [ ] Applications filed
- [ ] IP monitored
- [ ] IP maintained
- [ ] IP enforced
- [ ] IP integrated into business
- [ ] IP budget allocated

## 7. Crowdfunding & Prototyping Budgets

### 7.1 Crowdfunding Platforms

| Platform | Best for | Fees | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kickstarter** | Creative projects, products | 5% + payment processing | All-or-nothing funding |
| **Indiegogo** | Flexible funding, products | 5% + payment processing | Flexible or fixed funding |
| **GoFundMe** | Personal causes, emergencies | 0% platform fee | Donation-based |
| **Patreon** | Ongoing support, creators | 8-12% | Subscription-based |
| **SeedInvest** | Equity crowdfunding | 7% + legal fees | Equity investment |
| **Republic** | Equity crowdfunding | Varies | Equity investment |

### 7.2 Crowdfunding Budget Framework

**Pre-launch costs:**
- Product development: $10K-$100K+
- Prototype creation: $5K-$50K
- Photography/video: $2K-$10K
- Marketing materials: $2K-$10K
- Legal/fees: $1K-$5K
- **Total pre-launch:** $20K-$175K+

**Campaign costs:**
- Platform fees: 5-12% of funds raised
- Marketing/advertising: $5K-$50K
- Fulfillment/logistics: 20-30% of funds raised
- Rewards/thank-you gifts: 5-10% of funds raised
- **Total campaign costs:** 30-50% of funds raised

**Post-launch costs:**
- Production: 30-40% of funds raised
- Fulfillment: 20-30% of funds raised
- Customer service: 5-10% of funds raised
- Returns/refunds: 5-10% of funds raised
- **Total post-launch costs:** 60-90% of funds raised

### 7.3 Prototyping Budget Framework

| Prototype tier | Cost range | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Concept** | $0-$50 | Cardboard, paper, basic materials |
| **Muslin** | $50-$500 | Muslin fabric, basic sewing |
| **Sample** | $500-$5K | Target materials, hardware, production techniques |
| **PP Sample** | $5K-$50K | Production materials, methods, packaging |
| **Pilot** | $50K-$500K | Production run, market testing |
| **Mass Production** | $500K-$10M+ | Full production, retail launch |

### 7.4 Crowdfunding Checklist

- [ ] Product validated with customers
- [ ] Prototype ready for demonstration
- [ ] Campaign page created (story, rewards, video)
- [ ] Marketing plan developed
- [ ] Budget calculated (pre-launch, campaign, post-launch)
- [ ] Fulfillment plan created
- [ ] Legal requirements met
- [ ] Risk factors identified
- [ ] Timeline realistic
- [ ] Communication plan developed

## 8. Sustainability Claims That Hold Up

### 8.1 Sustainability Claim Types

| Claim type | What it means | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| **Recycled content** | Contains recycled materials | Third-party certification |
| **Organic** | Grown without pesticides | GOTS, OEKO-TEX certification |
| **Fair trade** | Fair labor practices | Fair Trade certification |
| **Carbon neutral** | Net zero carbon emissions | Carbon offset verification |
| **Biodegradable** | Decomposes naturally | Testing, certification |
| **Vegan** | No animal products | Material verification |
| **Cruelty-free** | No animal testing | Certification |
| **Local** | Produced locally | Supply chain transparency |
| **Circular** | Designed for recycling | End-of-life plan |
| **Regenerative** | Restores ecosystems | Impact assessment |

### 8.2 Sustainability Claim Verification

| Claim | How to verify | Red flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Recycled content** | Third-party certification (e.g., GRS) | No certification, vague claims |
| **Organic** | GOTS, OEKO-TEX certification | No certification, greenwashing |
| **Fair trade** | Fair Trade certification | No certification, vague claims |
| **Carbon neutral** | Carbon offset verification | No verification, greenwashing |
| **Biodegradable** | Testing, certification | No testing, vague claims |
| **Vegan** | Material verification | Hidden animal products |
| **Cruelty-free** | Certification | No certification, animal testing |
| **Local** | Supply chain transparency | Vague claims, no verification |
| **Circular** | End-of-life plan | No plan, greenwashing |
| **Regenerative** | Impact assessment | No assessment, greenwashing |

### 8.3 Sustainability Claim Checklist

- [ ] Claims are specific and measurable
- [ ] Claims are verified by third-party certification
- [ ] Claims are transparent (supply chain visible)
- [ ] Claims are accurate (no exaggeration)
- [ ] Claims are substantiated (data to back up)
- [ ] Claims are consistent (all products meet claim)
- [ ] Claims are communicated clearly
- [ ] Claims are updated regularly
- [ ] Greenwashing avoided
- [ ] Customer education provided

## 9. Competitive Analysis Framework

### 9.1 Competitive Analysis Components

| Component | What it covers | Data sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Market overview** | Market size, growth, trends | Industry reports, market data |
| **Competitor identification** | Who are the competitors? | Market research, industry knowledge |
| **Competitor profiles** | Strengths, weaknesses, strategies | Company websites, reports, reviews |
| **Product comparison** | Features, pricing, positioning | Product analysis, customer reviews |
| **Market share** | Who has what share? | Market data, sales estimates |
| **Pricing analysis** | Price points, strategies | Competitor websites, retail |
| **Distribution analysis** | Where are they sold? | Retail analysis, online presence |
| **Marketing analysis** | How do they market? | Advertising, social media, PR |
| **SWOT analysis** | Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats | Comprehensive analysis |

### 9.2 Competitive Analysis Process

1. **Define market** — what market are you in?
2. **Identify competitors** — who are your competitors?
3. **Gather data** — collect information on competitors
4. **Analyze data** — identify patterns, trends, insights
5. **Develop strategy** — how will you compete?
6. **Monitor** — track competitor moves, market changes

### 9.3 Competitive Analysis Checklist

- [ ] Market defined clearly
- [ ] Competitors identified comprehensively
- [ ] Data gathered from multiple sources
- [ ] Analysis completed systematically
- [ ] Insights documented
- [ ] Strategy developed based on analysis
- [ ] Monitoring plan established
- [ ] Analysis updated regularly
