# Material Science Guide for Fashion Innovation

Reference module for Fashion Genesis X. Covers fiber taxonomy, smart textiles, bio-based alternatives, recycled/upcycled systems, 3D-printed textiles, composites, and evaluation frameworks.

Status convention: **[VERIFIED]** = commercial/established, **[EXPERIMENTAL]** = demonstrated in labs or limited production, **[SPECULATIVE]** = conceptual or early research.

## 2. Smart Textiles

### 2.1 Phase-Change Materials (PCMs)

**What:** Microcapsules containing phase-change materials (paraffin, fatty acids) that absorb/release heat at specific temperatures.

**How it works:** At melting point, PCM absorbs heat (solid → liquid); at freezing point, releases heat (liquid → solid). Maintains thermal comfort zone.

**Applications:** Linings, insulation layers, footwear insoles, gloves.

**Examples:** Outlast (licensed technology), Thermo-Ecologe (used in some outdoor brands).

**Status:** [VERIFIED] — commercially available, used in military, outdoor, and medical textiles.

### 2.2 Thermochromic Textiles

**What:** Dyes/pigments that change color with temperature.

**Types:**
- **Liquid crystals:** precise temperature ranges, reversible.
- **Leuco dyes:** wider range, less precise, reversible.
- **Photochromic:** change with UV light exposure.

**Applications:** Mood rings, temperature indicators, fashion statements, safety wear.

**Examples:** Hypercolor (discontinued but iconic), Chameleon Collective (UK brand), ThermoBalloon (interactive art).

**Status:** [VERIFIED] — commercially available, niche market.

### 2.3 Photoluminescent Textiles

**What:** Materials that absorb light and emit it in dark (glow-in-the-dark).

**Types:**
- **Phosphorescent:** long-lasting glow (hours).
- **Fluorescent:** immediate glow under UV.
- **Electroluminescent:** light-emitting when powered.

**Applications:** Safety wear, fashion statements, signage, interactive art.

**Examples:** GlowSteps (UK safety footwear), Vollebak (phosphorescent details), Nike's photoluminescent running shoes.

**Status:** [VERIFIED] — commercially available, growing market.

### 2.4 Conductive Textiles

**What:** Fabrics with electrical conductivity for sensing, heating, communication.

**Types:**
- **Metallic fibers:** silver, copper, stainless steel yarns.
- **Conductive polymers:** PEDOT:PSS, polyaniline.
- **Carbon-based:** carbon fiber, graphene, carbon nanotubes.
- **Printed electronics:** conductive inks printed on fabric.

**Applications:** Heated clothing, biometric sensors, LED displays, smart gloves, interactive fashion.

**Examples:** Ministry of Supply (heated jacket), Google Jacquard (smart jacket, discontinued but influential), Ralph Lauren Polo Tech Shirt (2018), CuteCircuit (wearable technology).

**Status:** [VERIFIED] — commercially available, growing.

### 2.5 Shape-Memory Textiles

**What:** Materials that return to original shape after deformation (temperature, moisture, light trigger).

**Types:**
- **Shape-memory alloys (SMAs):** nickel-titanium (Nitinol).
- **Shape-memory polymers (SMPs):** polyurethane, polylactic acid.
- **Moisture-responsive:** hydrogels, cellulose-based.

**Applications:** Self-fitting garments, adaptive clothing, deployable structures, responsive fashion.

**Examples:** Yarns with Nitinol (research), Shape memory polymer fabrics (research), Self-tightening shoelaces (research).

**Status:** [EXPERIMENTAL] — demonstrated in labs, limited commercial use.

## 3. Bio-Based & Sustainable Alternatives

### 3.1 Lab-Grown Leather

**What:** Animal-free leather grown from cells in bioreactors.

**Companies:**
- **Modern Meadow:** collagen-based leather alternative (Zoa).
- **Bolt Threads:** Microsilk (spider silk), Mycelium leather.
- **VitroLabs:** lab-grown leather from animal cells.

**Properties:** Customizable, animal-free, potentially more sustainable.

**Challenges:** Scalability, cost, durability vs. traditional leather.

**Status:** [EXPERIMENTAL] — some products available, scaling ongoing.

### 3.2 Mycelium Leather

**What:** Fungal mycelium grown on agricultural waste substrate, processed into leather-like material.

**Companies:**
- **MycoWorks:** Fine Mycelium (used by Hermès for Victoria bag, 2021).
- **Bolt Threads:** Mycelium leather.
- **Ecovative:** mycelium-based materials.

**Properties:** Biodegradable, sustainable, leather-like texture, customizable.

**Challenges:** Durability, water resistance, scalability, consistency.

**Status:** [EXPERIMENTAL] — Hermès bag proved luxury viability; scaling ongoing.

### 3.4 Recycled & Upcycled Systems

**Recycled fibers:**
- **Recycled polyester (rPET):** PET bottles → fiber. [VERIFIED]
- **Recycled nylon (rNylon):** Fishing nets → fiber (ECONYL). [VERIFIED]
- **Recycled cotton:** Post-consumer cotton → fiber. [VERIFIED]
- **Recycled wool:** Pre/post-consumer wool → fiber. [VERIFIED]
- **Ocean plastic:** Ocean-bound plastic → fiber. [VERIFIED]

**Upcycled materials:**
- **Deadstock fabric:** Unsold fabric from factories. [VERIFIED]
- **Vintage clothing:** Reworked or resold. [VERIFIED]
- **Scrap fabric:** Offcuts → accessories, patchwork. [VERIFIED]
- **Textile waste:** Industrial waste → fiber. [VERIFIED]
- **Fashion waste:** Post-consumer clothing → fiber or products. [VERIFIED]

**Circular economy models:**
- **Take-back programs:** Brands collect used clothing (e.g., H&M, Patagonia). [VERIFIED]
- **Rental/repair services:** Extend garment life (e.g., Rent the Runway, The Renewal Workshop). [VERIFIED]
- **Resale platforms:** Depop, Poshmark, The RealReal. [VERIFIED]
- **Design for disassembly:** Garments designed for easy recycling. [EXPERIMENTAL]

### 3.5 3D-Printed Textiles

**What:** Textile structures created via 3D printing/additive manufacturing.

**Types:**
- **FDM printed textiles:** Fused deposition modeling → flexible structures.
- **SLS printed textiles:** Selective laser sintering → nylon structures.
- **SLA printed textiles:** Stereolithography → resin structures.
- **Multi-material printing:** Multiple materials in one print.

**Applications:** Custom-fit garments, lattice structures, embedded electronics, custom orthotics, experimental fashion.

**Examples:**
- **Danit Peleg:** 3D-printed gown (2015).
- **Noea:** 3D-printed shoes.
- **Continuum Fashion:** 3D-printed accessories.
- **Kornit Digital:** digital textile printing.

**Challenges:** Speed, material properties, comfort, scalability.

**Status:** [EXPERIMENTAL] — demonstrated in fashion, limited commercial use.

### 3.6 Composites for Wearable Structures

**What:** Materials combining fibers and matrix for enhanced properties.

**Types:**
- **Carbon fiber composites:** High strength-to-weight, stiff. [VERIFIED] — used in prosthetics, exoskeletons, performance footwear.
- **Fiberglass composites:** Strong, cheap, heavy. [VERIFIED] — used in helmets, protective gear.
- **Aramid (Kevlar) composites:** High strength, heat-resistant. [VERIFIED] — used in protective gear, cut-resistant gloves.
- **Natural fiber composites:** Flax, hemp, jute + bio-resin. [EXPERIMENTAL] — sustainable alternative.
- **Metal matrix composites:** Metal + ceramic/particle reinforcement. [EXPERIMENTAL] — used in luxury accessories.

**Applications:** Exoskeletons, prosthetics, protective gear, performance footwear, structural fashion.

**Status:** [VERIFIED] for carbon fiber/aramid; [EXPERIMENTAL] for natural fiber composites.

## 4. Material Evaluation Framework

### 4.1 Durability

| Test | What it measures | Standard methods |
|---|---|---|
| **Tensile strength** | Force to break fabric | ASTM D5034 |
| **Tear strength** | Force to tear fabric | ASTM D1424 |
| **Abrasion resistance** | Resistance to rubbing | ASTM D4966 (Martindale) |
| **Pilling resistance** | Resistance to fuzz formation | ASTM D4970 |
| **Seam strength** | Force to break seam | ASTM D1683 |
| **Colorfastness** | Resistance to fading | AATCC/ISO standards |
| **Dimensional stability** | Resistance to shrinkage/growth | AATCC/ISO standards |

### 4.2 Hand-Feel (Drape & Handle)

| Property | What it means | How to evaluate |
|---|---|---|
| **Drape** | How fabric falls and moves | Hang test, drape coefficient |
| **Handle** | Subjective feel (soft, crisp, stiff, smooth) | Touch test, Kawabata Evaluation System |
| **Bending stiffness** | Resistance to bending | Kawabata bending test |
| **Shear stiffness** | Resistance to twisting | Kawabata shear test |
| **Compression** | Softness under pressure | Kawabata compression test |
| **Surface friction** | Smoothness/roughness | Kawabata friction test |

### 4.3 Care Requirements

| Factor | What to consider |
|---|---|
| **Washability** | Machine wash, hand wash, dry clean only |
| **Drying** | Tumble dry, line dry, flat dry |
| **Ironing** | Required, optional, delicate |
| **Stain resistance** | Easy to clean, requires special care |
| **Shrinkage** | Pre-shrunk, requires careful washing |
| **Pilling** | Requires de-pilling, avoid friction |

### 4.4 Toxicity & Safety

| Concern | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Azo dyes** | Banned in EU, restricted elsewhere |
| **Formaldehyde** | Finishes, resins — skin irritant |
| **Heavy metals** | Dyes, pigments — toxic |
| **Phthalates** | PVC, prints — endocrine disruptor |
| **PFAS** | Waterproofing — persistent pollutant |
| **Allergens** | Latex, nickel, certain dyes |
| **Flame retardants** | Some are toxic, restricted |
| **Microplastics** | Synthetic fibers shed in washing |

### 4.5 Sustainability Metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Carbon footprint** | CO2 emissions per unit |
| **Water usage** | Liters per unit |
| **Energy usage** | kWh per unit |
| **Waste generation** | Waste per unit produced |
| **Recycled content** | % recycled material |
| **Biodegradability** | Time to decompose |
| **Recyclability** | Can be recycled at end of life |
| **Renewable content** | % bio-based material |
| **Chemical usage** | Dyes, finishes, treatments |
| **Social impact** | Labor practices, working conditions |

### 4.6 Commercial vs. Experimental vs. Speculative

| Label | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial** | Available for purchase, used in products | Cotton, polyester, Gore-Tex, Repreve |
| **Experimental** | Demonstrated in labs or limited production | Mycelium leather, lab-grown leather, 3D-printed textiles |
| **Speculative** | Conceptual or early research | Algae-based fibers, quantum dot textiles, advanced biomaterials |

## QUICK REFERENCE

**Fiber selection:**
- Natural = breathable, biodegradable, renewable
- Synthetic = durable, cheap, moisture-wicking, recyclable
- Regenerated = soft, biodegradable, closed-loop possible
- Experimental = innovative, sustainable, limited availability

**Smart textile applications:**
- PCMs = thermal comfort
- Thermochromic = color change, safety
- Photoluminescent = glow, visibility
- Conductive = sensing, heating, displays
- Shape-memory = adaptive, self-fitting

**Sustainability priority:**
1. Recycled > virgin
2. Natural > synthetic (if renewable)
3. Biodegradable > non-biodegradable
4. Local > global (transport emissions)
5. Durable > disposable

**Evaluation checklist:**
- [ ] Durability tested (tensile, tear, abrasion)
- [ ] Hand-feel acceptable (drape, handle)
- [ ] Care requirements practical
- [ ] Toxicity safe (no banned substances)
- [ ] Sustainability assessed (carbon, water, waste)
- [ ] Cost viable for target market
- [ ] Scalability confirmed
- [ ] IP/patent status checked

**Status labels:**
- [VERIFIED] = commercially available, proven
- [EXPERIMENTAL] = demonstrated, limited production
- [SPECULATIVE] = conceptual, early research
