# MODE G — COLLECTION CREATION

## PURPOSE

Design a complete, coherent fashion collection — a creative universe where every
product shares recognizable DNA. Output is a creative-direction document plus
individual product concepts, ready to feed image generation and commercialization.

## TRIGGERS

- "design a collection", "create a line", "whole range", "collection for [brand/
  concept/mood]", "seasonal direction", "capsule collection"

## CORE PRINCIPLE

A collection is NOT a list of products. It is ONE idea expressed across many
objects. If you can remove any single item and nothing changes, the collection
has no spine. Build the spine first (manifesto + DNA), then populate it.

---

## PROCEDURE

### STEP 1 — Creative manifesto

Write 1 paragraph (3–6 sentences) stating:
- What the collection BELIEVES (the worldview, not the products)
- Who it serves and in what moment of their life
- What it refuses to be (the anti-positioning — equally important)

Test: could this manifesto produce clothing, footwear, AND bags? If it only
works for one category, sharpen it until it generalizes.

Name the collection. Names should be conceptual, not descriptive
("TERRA FIRMA" not "Earth Collection 2026").

### STEP 2 — Central inspiration + philosophy

State the single central inspiration (a place, material, system, tension, era,
body state — NOT a mood word like "futuristic"). Then articulate the design
philosophy in 2–3 sentences: how the inspiration becomes form.

### STEP 3 — DNA extraction (THE critical step)

Define exactly **3 signature traits** that EVERY item in the collection shares.
These are the collection's DNA. Each trait must be:

- **Visible** (detectable in product form, not just concept)
- **Translatable** across categories (works on a dress, a boot, a tote)
- **Specific** (not "minimalist" — too vague; "single curved seam replacing all
  darts and pockets" — testable)

Write each DNA trait as: trait name · what it looks like · what it does
(functional justification). A DNA trait with no function is decoration — reject.

Example DNA:
1. **Monolith fold** — garments are cut from single curved panels; no shoulder
   seams, no side seams. Looks sculptural; functionally eliminates seam stress
   points and enables one-piece repair.
2. **Living fastener** — every opening uses the same magnetic-mechanical clasp
   family, scaled per product. Function: one-hand operation, tool-free repair.
3. **Soil palette logic** — colors chosen as if dyed in earth/stone/mineral
   pigments; colors interact by tonal adjacency, never complementary contrast.
   Function: colors age together, stain camouflage, low-dye-impact story.

### STEP 4 — Visual identity language

Derive from DNA:
- **Silhouette language** — the 3–4 silhouette archetypes allowed (e.g.
  column, cocoon, asymmetric wrap, architectural fold). Every product must be
  describable as a variation of one of these.
- **Surface/texture language** — how light interacts: matte, rippled, brushed,
  transparent zones.
- **Detail grammar** — where hardware sits, how edges finish, whether topstitching
  is visible.

### STEP 5 — Material palette

Select 4–6 materials maximum. For each: name, role (primary/secondary/trim),
why it serves the DNA, sustainability note. Every material must earn its place
against at least one DNA trait. Flag any material with unresolved supply or
safety questions.

### STEP 6 — Color system

Define:
- Core palette: 4–6 colors with names and hex approximations (label as
  approximations if not spec'd)
- Color logic rule (e.g. tonal adjacency, one accent reserved for hardware)
- Color-per-category mapping if relevant

### STEP 7 — Product lineup architecture

Structure the collection as:

```
HERO PRODUCT (1)        — the invention that carries the manifesto;
                          must be the strongest single product in the line
SUPPORTING PRODUCTS     — 5–9 items across categories (apparel, footwear,
                          bags, accessories as fits the manifesto)
                          Each must express all 3 DNA traits.
TRANSITION/STORY PIECES — optional 1–3 items that extend the world
                          (packaging, textile objects, digital layer)
```

For EACH product deliver a condensed concept block (NOT full A–M — that's for
the hero):
- Product name + category
- How each of the 3 DNA traits appears in THIS product
- One signature mechanism or construction detail
- Material from palette + color(s)
- One-line user story

The hero product gets the full Master Format A–M treatment.

### STEP 8 — Styling direction

Define how pieces combine: layering rules, proportion rules, what can/can't be
mixed. Include 3–4 named outfit/ styling formulas (e.g. "The Architect:
hero outer + column trouser + monolith-fold tote").

### STEP 9 — Editorial campaign concept

One paragraph: the visual world of the campaign — location logic, casting
direction, photography style, key campaign image descriptions (3). The campaign
must make the DNA visible without explaining it.

### STEP 10 — Commercial positioning

- Target customer (specific, not "fashion-forward women")
- Price architecture: entry / core / hero tiers (ranges, labeled estimates)
- Channel logic: where it's sold and why
- The one-sentence sell: what the collection IS in a phrase a buyer repeats

### STEP 11 — Consistency audit (MANDATORY)

Run the checklist below against EVERY product before delivery. Any product
missing a DNA trait gets redesigned or cut. A collection with a weak member
teaches the customer the DNA is optional.

---

## CONSISTENCY CHECKLIST (run per product)

- [ ] Product name fits the collection's naming logic
- [ ] All 3 DNA traits visibly present (state HOW for each)
- [ ] Silhouette maps to an allowed archetype
- [ ] Materials drawn only from the palette
- [ ] Colors drawn only from the color system
- [ ] Detail grammar respected (hardware placement, edge finishes)
- [ ] Product category justified by manifesto (not added to fill a grid)
- [ ] Functions serve the manifesto's user, not generic use
- [ ] No product is a "reskin" of an external reference
- [ ] Hero product is genuinely the strongest item, not just the biggest

## COLLECTION-LEVEL CHECKLIST

- [ ] Manifesto passes the cross-category test
- [ ] DNA traits are visible, translatable, and specific
- [ ] Every DNA trait has a functional justification
- [ ] Material palette ≤6, each with a role
- [ ] Color system has an explicit logic rule
- [ ] Lineup has 1 hero + 5–9 supporting minimum
- [ ] Every category claimed by the manifesto has ≥1 product
- [ ] Styling formulas demonstrate mix-and-match logic
- [ ] Campaign concept shows DNA without explaining it
- [ ] Price tiers cover entry→hero without contradiction
- [ ] No two products share the same silhouette archetype unless differentiated
      by DNA expression

---

## ANTI-PATTERNS (reject immediately)

- DNA traits that are moods ("ethereal", "bold") instead of visible properties
- A "collection" that is one hero product plus generic basics
- Every product in the same silhouette (looks like a uniform, not a collection)
- Materials or colors introduced for one product only (breaks the system)
- Hero product that doesn't express the DNA most clearly — wrong hero
- Category stuffing (shoes + bags + dresses + eyewear) when the manifesto only
  supports 3 categories

---

## HANDOFF NOTES

- The hero product's full A–M output is the anchor document; supporting product
  blocks intentionally stay condensed — deepen any of them on request via
  `product-innovation.md`.
- If the user wants visuals next, route to `workflows/image-generation.md`:
  hero product gets all 10 views; supporting products get views 1, 2, 5, 6, 7.
- If the user wants business framing, route to `workflows/commercialization.md`
  using the hero product as the commercial object and the manifesto as the
  positioning foundation.
- If the collection contains mechanism-heavy items (closures, transforms),
  flag them for `workflows/product-development.md` — they need tier-ladder
  prototyping, not just spec sheets.

## OUTPUT CHECKLIST

- [ ] Manifesto + name + inspiration + philosophy delivered
- [ ] Exactly 3 DNA traits defined with visible/function/specification detail
- [ ] Silhouette language, material palette (≤6), color system defined
- [ ] Lineup: 1 hero (full A–M) + 5–9 supporting (concept blocks)
- [ ] Styling direction + 3–4 outfit formulas
- [ ] Editorial campaign concept + commercial positioning
- [ ] Consistency audit run per product; failures fixed or cut
- [ ] Collection-level checklist passed
- [ ] No fabricated prices/certifications; estimates labeled
- [ ] Offer next steps: image generation for hero, product specs, or
      commercialization pass
