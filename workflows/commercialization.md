# COMMERCIALIZATION — BUSINESS ENGINE WORKFLOW

## PURPOSE

Turn an invention into a business case: market intelligence, customer definition,
positioning, pricing hypothesis, MVP definition, launch narrative, IP strategy,
and a 90-day validation plan. Output is a commercial brief — honest about
unknowns, rigorous about assumptions.

## TRIGGERS

- "how do we sell this", "business case", "commercialize", "market this",
  "pricing", "launch", "who buys this", "patent", "IP strategy", "MVP"
- Follow-up after invention/product-development when the user wants to go to market.

## GOLDEN RULES

1. **Never fabricate numbers.** No invented market sizes, prices, conversion
   rates, or competitor revenues. Give ranges with stated assumptions, or say
   "requires research."
2. **Label every estimate.** Format: `estimate: $X–Y (assumes A, B; region R;
   date D)`.
3. **Distinguish fact from hypothesis.** Structure output so the user can see
   what's known vs. what's being tested.
4. **IP claims need research.** Never assert patentability/freedom-to-operate;
   recommend professional search. Label preliminary audits.

---

## STEP 1 — Market intelligence gathering

Collect what the invention competes with and where it sits.

### 1a. Category scan
- Identify the incumbent category(ies) the invention disrupts or creates.
- List 3–5 incumbent products/brands with price tiers (public info only).
- Note the category's price architecture: entry / mid / premium / luxury bands.

### 1b. Demand signals (qualitative)
- Who currently solves this problem and how (including non-product solutions:
  workarounds, services, tolerance of the problem)?
- What adjacent categories does the invention borrow users from?
- Any observable trend supporting the timing (search interest, cultural moment,
  regulatory shift)? Label as signal, not proof.

### 1c. Gap statement
Write one sentence: "Incumbents in [category] fail [user] by [specific failure],
and no product currently does [invention's core job]." If you can't write this
honestly, the invention may be a differentiation, not a new market — say so.

## STEP 2 — Customer persona template

Produce ONE primary persona (not three shallow ones). Fill every field:

```
PERSONA: [Name placeholder] — [archetype]
DEMOGRAPHIC: age range, location type, income band (ranges, not false precision)
CONTEXT: the recurring situation where the problem/invention matters
CURRENT BEHAVIOR: what they do today instead (the real competition)
PAIN COST: what the problem costs them (time, money, comfort, status, health)
TRIGGER MOMENT: what makes them decide to buy NOW
OBJECTION #1: the strongest reason they won't buy (price? unfamiliarity?
              durability doubt?) — design the launch against this
OBJECTION #2: second barrier
SUCCESS IMAGE: what "it worked" looks like for them
WHERE THEY SHOP: channels they actually use (not where you'd like them to)
```

Rules:
- Ground the persona in the invention's actual user — don't invent a market
  to fit the product.
- "Current behavior" is the real competitor; include doing-nothing as an option.
- If the invention serves a niche (adaptive, medical-adjacent, subculture),
  say so — niche ≠ weak, but pricing/distribution logic changes.

## STEP 3 — Positioning decision

Choose ONE positioning frame and defend it:

| Frame | When it fits | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Category creator** ("new product type") | Invention does a genuinely new job | Education cost; buyers must learn the category |
| **Category disruptor** ("better X") | Invention improves a known category | Competes on incumbent terms |
| **Premium/luxury** | Craft/material/story justify price | Narrow market, high bar |
| **Accessible innovation** | Mechanism is simple, price-sensitive users | Margin pressure, volume needed |
| **Professional/specialist** | Serves work/sport/medical context | Smaller TAM, loyal base |
| **Cultural/status object** | Design is the product | Fashion-cycle risk, hype-dependent |

Deliver:
- Chosen frame + one-sentence positioning statement:
  "For [persona], [product] is the [frame] that [core benefit], unlike
  [alternative], because [mechanism/proof]."
- What the positioning REQUIRES (e.g. category-creator needs education budget;
  luxury needs proof of craft).
- What it FORBIDS (e.g. can't discount, can't sell in fast-fashion channels).

## STEP 4 — Pricing hypothesis framework

NEVER state a price as fact. Build a hypothesis with visible logic:

### 4a. Cost floor
- From product-development.md cost analysis: unit cost range at target volume.
- Add margin requirement for the chosen channel (DTC needs higher margin than
  wholesale; estimate channel margin ranges, labeled).

### 4b. Value ceiling
- What does the incumbent alternative cost? (the price anchor)
- What is the invention's value delta — what does the extra function/design
  justify? Quantify if possible (time saved, problem eliminated, durability).

### 4c. Price band hypothesis
```
PRICE HYPOTHESIS: $X–Y (estimate)
Logic: cost floor $A–B + channel margin C% → floor ~$F
       value anchor: incumbent $G; delta justifies up to ~$H
       chosen band: $X–Y sits between, leaning [premium/mass]
ASSUMPTIONS: [volume, channel mix, material costs, no exotic tooling]
TEST: this is a hypothesis — validate via pre-orders, landing-page tests,
      or concierge sales before committing.
```

### 4d. Tier architecture (if applicable)
- Entry product (access the brand/world at lower price)
- Core product (the hero at target margin)
- Signature/luxury tier (price ceiling test)

## STEP 5 — MVP product definition

Define the minimum sellable version — NOT the minimum feature set, the minimum
thing that tests the business hypothesis:

```
MVP DEFINITION
Hypothesis being tested: [e.g. "users will pay $X for [core job]"]
MVP contains: [features/mechanisms that deliver the core job — nothing else]
MVP excludes: [everything else, explicitly — this list matters]
Known compromises: [what's fake, manual, or ugly in MVP]
Success metric: [what result validates the hypothesis — e.g. "10 paid
  pre-orders at $X within 30 days"]
Kill criteria: [what result means STOP — e.g. "<3 pre-orders after 200
  qualified visits"]
```

Rules:
- The MVP must deliver the CORE JOB completely — a half-working mechanism
  tests nothing.
- Manual/back-end work is fine in MVP; fake product is not.
- One hypothesis per MVP. If you're testing pricing AND demand AND mechanism,
  you've designed three experiments, not one.

## STEP 6 — Launch narrative

Write the story the market receives:

- **Origin line:** why this exists (the problem, observed honestly — not
  fabricated founder mythology).
- **Invention reveal:** the "how did they think of this" moment — one
  mechanism explained plainly.
- **Enemy:** what it's against (planned obsolescence? discomfort? ugly
  compromises? category stagnation?) — name it specifically.
- **Proof:** what evidence exists today (prototype photos, test results,
  material samples) vs. what's promised.
- **Invitation:** what the customer does first (join waitlist, preorder,
  visit pop-up).

Rules:
- No fabricated credentials, awards, certifications, or testimonials.
- "Proof" section must distinguish demonstrated vs. claimed.
- The narrative should make the DESIGN the story — if the story is better
  than the product, that's a warning.

## STEP 7 — IP route selection

Lay out the options honestly — this is decision support, not legal advice.
Recommend professional counsel before filing anything.

| Route | Protects | Typical cost range | Timeline | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Design patent** | ornamental appearance | $1k–15k+ (USPTO fees + attorney) | 12–24 mo | distinctive visual design |
| **Utility patent** | functional mechanism | $10k–50k+ | 2–5 yrs | novel mechanism, material process |
| **Provisional patent** | placeholder, 12-month pendency | $1k–5k | immediate | buying time before full filing |
| **Trademark** | name/logo | $250–1k/class + attorney | 8–14 mo | brand name (file EARLY — first-to-file) |
| **Trade dress** | product look/function IF distinctive+nonfunctional | litigation-costly | years | iconic product shapes (rare, hard) |
| **Copyright** | artistic elements | cheap | months | prints, graphics (not useful for 3D products) |
| **Trade secret** | processes/materials | ~$0 | indefinite | manufacturing methods hard to reverse-engineer |

Decision framework:
1. Is the value in the MECHANISM? → provisional patent NOW to preserve date,
   then decide on full utility.
2. Is the value in the LOOK? → design patent + trademark on name.
3. Is the value in the NAME? → trademark file immediately (cheap, first-to-file).
4. Is the manufacturing process the moat? → trade secret, strict NDA discipline.
5. Multiple routes usually apply — recommend a combined strategy.

Always include:
- **Preliminary freedom-to-operate note:** what categories of prior art to
  search (patent classes, competitor products, academic literature).
- **Disclosure warning:** public disclosure before filing can forfeit patent
  rights in most jurisdictions — flag this BEFORE any launch/PR.
- **Recommendation to hire IP counsel** for actual filing decisions.

## STEP 8 — 90-day validation plan

Structure as weekly milestones with kill/pivot criteria.

```
WEEKS 1–2: CONCEPT VALIDATION
- Landing page or concierge offer live
- 50–100 targeted outreach conversations (not surveys — conversations)
- Measure: problem resonance (do they describe the pain unprompted?),
  willingness-to-pay signal (would they pre-order at $X?)
- Gate: ≥30% express strong interest → proceed; <10% → pivot problem/positioning

WEEKS 3–6: MVP BUILD + SOFT LAUNCH
- MVP per Step 5 built
- Soft launch to warm audience (email list, community, niche press)
- Measure: conversion rate, pre-order count, qualitative feedback themes
- Gate: hit pre-order target → proceed to production planning;
  miss → diagnose (price? product? audience?) before spending more

WEEKS 7–10: PRODUCTION PATH + PRICING TEST
- Get 2–3 manufacturer quotes (validates cost model)
- Test price points via pre-order page variants or direct offers
- Measure: quote vs. cost hypothesis, price elasticity signal
- Gate: unit economics work at SOME price point → proceed;
  no viable price → redesign cost structure or reposition

WEEKS 11–13: DECISION
- Compile: demand signal, cost reality, margin at viable price, qualitative
  response to prototype
- Decision: GO (production run planning) / PIVOT (change segment, price,
  or product scope) / STOP (kill criteria met — say so honestly)
```

Rules:
- Every gate has a NUMBER, not a vibe.
- Budget honesty: state estimated cash needed per phase (ranges).
- The plan must include a STOP path — validation that can't kill the project
  isn't validation.
