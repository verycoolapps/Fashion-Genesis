# REVOLUTIONARY CLOTHING — Mode H: Full-Spectrum Invention

## A. Invention Reveal

**Name:** MERIDIAN SKELETON — a three-mode variable-structure jacket system.

**The one-sentence reveal:** A jacket whose outer silhouette is actively reconfigured by an internal tension network of Dyneema cables and shape-memory alloy (SMA) micro-actuators, switching between **Commute**, **Commute+Rain**, and **Storm Shell** wear states in under 4 seconds — with every reconfiguration producing a structurally different garment, not just a different look.

**The first image:** A figure on a rain-slicked Amsterdam street at 8:12 AM, wearing what reads as a tailored charcoal commuter coat. Water beads on the shoulder — and the coat visibly inhales: the hem drops two inches, the shoulders square into a storm profile, the sleeves migrate from tapered-trouser to articulated-armature, the back vents deploy from invisible fabric to structural channels. It is mid-transition. The person does not touch a zipper.

**The 60-second reveal:** The jacket is two garments sharing one skeleton. Its torso is a laminated "frame panel" structure — rigid enough to hold architectural lines, flexible enough to fold. Inside runs a web of 14 Dyneema® SK-78 cables (0.8 mm) arranged as a miniature suspension bridge, anchored at collar, shoulder, elbow, hip, and hem. A micro-pneumatic actuator pack at the left interior chest adjusts cable tension in six zones. Outer layers — a technical wool twill for Commute, a waterproof membrane bonded to the same shell for Rain — are mounted on the frame as "skins" that deploy, retract, or cloak depending on mode. When you select Storm Shell, the jacket doesn't add a layer: it **grows a skeleton** — shoulders extend 22 mm, sleeves lock into rigid load paths, and the hem's internal structure engages its drip-channel geometry.

**Why it feels new:** Every "transformable" jacket on the market does one of two things — zip open or zip shut. This one has a **third state of material reality**: the garment's internal architecture changes shape. Commute mode is a fitted coat optimized for sitting in transit. Rain mode is the same coat with a deployable membrane and adjusted hem clearance. Storm mode is a different garment entirely — one that treats the body as a load-bearing structure.

## B. Big Idea

**What's actually being invented:** A **variable-geometry garment** — a wearable structure whose shape is a computed output of tension distribution across a cable network, not a fixed pattern.

The conceptual leap: fashion treats "silhouette" as a 2D pattern problem (cut the fabric differently for different looks). We treat it as a **3D structural problem** — the jacket's form emerges from how its internal cables pull against its frame panels. Change the tension map, change the body.

**Why this could matter:**

- **Layer elimination.** The average commuter carries 3 garments: coat, rain shell, packable down. MERIDIAN replaces all three with one 640 g jacket (estimated). The value isn't "one jacket that does everything" — it's one jacket that **reconfigures its material properties** for each condition.
- **Weather as interface.** Rain isn't a threat to route around; it's a mode to enter. The jacket reads humidity and barometric pressure and can *suggest* (not force) mode transitions — a wrist haptic tap signals "storm approaching, switch?"
- **Durability through redundancy.** The cable skeleton is the garment's immune system. If a skin tears, the structure survives. If a cable frays (we instrument for this), it can be replaced in 90 seconds with a hand tool — the jacket is designed to be repaired like a bicycle, not discarded like fast fashion.

**The category it could create:** "Morphodynamic apparel" — clothing that changes its structural geometry in response to environmental or user input. Not smart clothing (electronics glued on) and not adaptive clothing (fabric that only stretches). This is **load-path manipulation** — the same physics as a tensegrity structure, scaled to human wear.

**Why now:** Three enabling technologies just converged — sub-millimeter Dyneema® braids cheap enough for consumer goods, micro-pneumatic actuators small enough to embed in a chest pack, and 3D-printed lattice structures that make custom cable anchors economically viable at low volume. The science existed for a decade; the supply chain didn't.

### The Specific Novelty

1. **Cable network as primary structure.** The jacket's silhouette is not pattern-cut; it is the emergent shape of a tensioned cable web interacting with semi-rigid frame panels. No other consumer garment uses tension distribution as its primary form-giving mechanism.

2. **Three distinct structural states.** Commute (relaxed drape), Rain (extended hem, deployed membrane), Storm (rigid load paths, extended shoulders). Each state has different mechanical properties — not just different coverage.

3. **Environmental sensing without electronics on the body.** A chest-mounted sensor pod (removable, 18 g) reads humidity, pressure, and temperature. It doesn't control the jacket — it *suggests* via haptic feedback. The human decides. This avoids the "smart clothing that betrays you" problem.

4. **Field-repairable skeleton.** Every cable anchor is a standardized 4 mm hex bolt. A frayed cable can be replaced with a $2 part and a multitool. The jacket is designed for a 10-year lifespan, not a season.

### The Gap

The gap between "fixed-geometry outerwear" and "true morphodynamic apparel" is that nobody has solved the **actuation problem at consumer price points**. Research labs have cable-driven morphing structures; they require lab equipment. MERIDIAN solves it with a $40 micro-pneumatic actuator pack and 14 pre-tensioned cables — crude, effective, repairable.

## F. Functional Mechanism

### The Tension Network

The jacket's silhouette is determined by **14 Dyneema® cables** arranged in a specific topology:

```
        Collar
       /      \
  Shoulder    Shoulder
     |          |
   Elbow      Elbow
     |          |
    Hip        Hip
       \      /
        Hem
```

Each cable is anchored at two points and tensioned by the actuator pack. The tension value determines the distance between anchor points — which determines the garment's shape.

### Actuation System

A micro-pneumatic pump (12V, 180 g) sits in a chest pocket. It drives air through 6 micro-valves to 6 pneumatic cylinders, each connected to a cable tensioner. The system can adjust tension in 12 discrete steps per zone.

**Mode Transitions:**

- **Commute → Rain:** Hem cables release tension (hem drops), back vent cables increase tension (vents open), membrane deploys via shape-memory alloy strips.
- **Rain → Storm:** Shoulder cables increase tension (shoulders extend), elbow cables lock (sleeves rigidify), hem cables re-tension with different geometry (drip channels engage).
- **Storm → Commute:** All cables return to baseline tension, skins retract, structure softens.

### Sensor Integration

A chest-mounted pod (18 g, removable) contains:
- **Humidity sensor** (SHT31): detects rain approaching
- **Barometer** (BMP388): detects pressure drops (storm incoming)
- **Accelerometer** (LIS2DH12): detects user motion (sitting, walking, running)
- **Temperature sensor**: detects body/ambient temp differential

The pod communicates with the actuator via Bluetooth Low Energy. It **suggests** mode transitions via haptic feedback (a tap pattern on the wristband). The user confirms or ignores. The jacket never auto-transitions — that would be dangerous (imagine it storming mid-commute and locking your arms).

### Biomechanical Honesty

The jacket does NOT:
- Improve athletic performance (it's not exoskeleton)
- Replace insulation (it's a shell, not a mid-layer)
- Work in temperatures below -10°C (the frame is not insulated)

It DOES:
- Reduce the number of layers needed (1 jacket vs. 3)
- Adapt to weather conditions faster than manual layering
- Provide structural protection in storm conditions (the rigid load paths distribute impact)
- Extend garment lifespan through repairability

## I. Manufacturing Strategy

### Prototype Phase (0–100 units)

- **Frame:** 3D-printed PA12 anchors, hand-assembled Dyneema® cables, off-the-shelf pneumatic components.
- **Skins:** Sourced from existing technical textile suppliers (wool twill, PU membrane).
- **Assembly:** Manual, in a small workshop. Estimated 4 hours per unit.
- **Cost:** ~$600 per unit at prototype volume.

### Low-Volume Production (100–1,000 units)

- **Frame:** CNC-machined aluminum anchors (stronger, lighter than 3D-printed).
- **Actuators:** Custom micro-pneumatic pump, integrated with valve manifold.
- **Skins:** Custom-woven wool twill, laser-cut membrane patterns.
- **Assembly:** Semi-automated — cables tensioned by jig, skins bonded by heat press.
- **Cost:** ~$450 per unit.

### Mass Production (1,000+ units)

- **Frame:** Injection-molded nylon anchors, automated cable threading.
- **Actuators:** MEMS-based micro-pneumatic system (smaller, cheaper).
- **Skins:** Roll-to-roll manufacturing, automated cutting/laminating.
- **Assembly:** Automated cable tensioning, robotic skin bonding.
- **Cost:** ~$300 per unit.

### Supply Chain

- **Dyneema®:** DSM (Netherlands) — sole supplier of SK-78 at consumer prices.
- **Wool twill:** Loro Piana, Vitale Barberis Canonico (Italy).
- **PU membrane:** Gore, Schoeller (Switzerland/Germany).
- **Pneumatic components:** Festo, SMC (Germany/Japan).
- **3D printing:** Carbon, Formlabs (US) for prototypes; local CNC for low volume.

### Quality Control

- **Cable tension testing:** Every unit tested at 6 tension points before shipment.
- **Waterproof testing:** 20,000 mm hydrostatic head test on every unit.
- **Mode transition testing:** Automated cycle test — 100 transitions per unit.
- **Durability testing:** 10,000 flex cycles on frame, 1,000 mode transitions.

**What likely exists that we're not aware of:**
- Academic papers on wearable tensegrity (likely exist, not commercialized)
- Patent applications for cable-driven garments (likely exist, not granted)

**What's actually different:**
1. **Consumer-viable actuation** — micro-pneumatic system at $40 cost (vs. lab equipment)
